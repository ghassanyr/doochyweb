Good concept — but the charts and asset coverage are the easy 20%. The hard 80% is deciding what “correct” means, keeping users from memorizing famous charts, and not accidentally training gamblers. Here’s how I’d think about it.
1. The core loop
At bar T, show a context window (say 60–120 candles), state the pattern, hide the future, then ask one of:
- Direction: up / down / no-trade
- Probability: “70% chance this breaks upward”
- Trade plan: entry, stop, target, then grade in R multiples
- Identification: “What pattern is this?” (name it before predicting)
- Validity: “Is this a valid flag, a near-miss, or noise?”
Then reveal, animate the next bars, draw the annotations, and show what actually happened plus the historical base rate for that setup.
The best version separates recognition from prediction: first “is this a real pattern?”, then “what’s the probability it resolves up?”, then “would you take the trade?”. Most trainers collapse all three into a binary up/down guess, which teaches almost nothing.
2. What “comprehensive” actually means
It’s a matrix, not a feature list:
Axis	Coverage
Assets	Crypto majors/alts, XAU/XAG/XPT, forex majors + crosses
Timeframes	1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 1D, 1W
Patterns	Candlesticks, chart patterns, market structure, volume, momentum, harmonics, Wyckoff/Elliott, ICT concepts, volatility regimes
Question types	Direction, probability, validity, identification, trade planning, “spot the fakeout”
Regimes	Trend, range, high/low volatility, news shock, session open
Example quality	Textbook, ambiguous, near-miss, negative control (random walk)
That last row is critical. A comprehensive trainer must include bad and ambiguous examples, not just perfect textbook setups. Otherwise you train confirmation bias.
Pattern taxonomy I’d aim for eventually:
- Candlestick: pin bar, engulfing, harami, morning/evening star, three soldiers/crows, inside/outside bar, doji
- Chart: H&S, double/triple top, triangles, flags, pennants, wedges, rectangles, cup & handle, rounding bottom, diamond
- Structure: HH/HL, BOS, CHoCH, S/R flip, trendline break, liquidity sweep, false breakout, retest
- Volume: climax, dry-up, divergence, breakout confirmation, VSA
- Momentum: RSI/MACD divergence, MA crossover, Bollinger squeeze, overbought/oversold
- ICT/price action: order block, FVG, breaker, mitigation, premium/discount, Judas swing, OTE
- Harmonic: Gartley, Bat, Butterfly, Crab, Shark, Cypher, AB=CD
- Wyckoff/Elliott: accumulation/distribution, spring/upthrust, impulse/correction
- Regime/event: gaps, session opens, news spikes, volatility compression/expansion
3. The answer-key problem (the crux)
“Guess the next move” is under-specified. Next candle? Next 5? Until the pattern invalidates? A single up/down label is noisy enough that users learn superstition rather than skill.
I’d use triple-barrier labeling (López de Prado style):
- At cut bar T, set an upper barrier at +k×ATR, a lower barrier at −k×ATR, and a time barrier at H bars.
- Label the instance by which barrier is hit first: up, down, or timeout/scratch.
- Store MFE/MAE (max favorable/adverse excursion) for richer feedback.
This makes the question gradeable and comparable across assets because it’s ATR-normalized, not raw dollars or pips. It also gives you a natural “no-trade” class, which is essential.
Then decide the philosophy:
- Arcade mode: binary direction, streaks, speed. Fun, but statistically misleading.
- Pro mode: probability + confidence, scored with Brier/log loss, plus trade planning in R. Teaches calibration and risk.
I’d ship both, but be explicit about which is the toy and which is the training.
4. Anti-memorization and statistical honesty
Users will recognize famous charts, reverse-image-search them, or just remember “the COVID crash one.” Countermeasures:
- Hide symbol, date, and absolute price; normalize the y-axis to % or index 100.
- Render as interactive vector charts, not images.
- Randomize cut point within the pattern’s development (early, mid, breakout, retest).
- Vertical flip charts occasionally (swap up/down and flip labels) — doubles the dataset and defeats memorization.
- Include placebo charts built from random walks or shuffled returns. Measure users’ false-positive rate. This is a killer pedagogical feature and a real differentiator.
- Never send future bars to the client before the answer. Serve context, validate server-side, then fetch the reveal. Otherwise anyone can inspect the network tab.
- Exclude or transform iconic events; add slight noise/jitter and alternative themes.
On scoring: one correct guess proves nothing. Use:
- Brier score for probability answers, and a skill score vs. the base rate.
- Item-response theory or Elo per pattern tag, so difficulty is estimated from the crowd, not hand-waved.
- Confidence intervals; don’t show a “92% accuracy” badge after 12 questions.
- Leaderboards ranked by skill, not raw accuracy, with a minimum sample size.
- Discard items everyone gets right or everyone gets wrong — they have no discriminative power.
This is also the ethical core: markets are noisy, and a trainer that implies “pattern = certainty” is harmful. The app should repeatedly say “this setup historically resolves up 54% of the time, which may not be an edge after costs.”
5. UX and modes
Core screens:
1. Chart stage — clean candles, optional volume, drawing tools, zoom/pan restricted to the past.
2. Answer panel — big up/down/pass buttons, confidence slider, optional probability input.
3. Reveal — animated future bars, auto-drawn pattern boundaries, invalidation level, MFE/MAE, base-rate stats, and a short “why.”
4. Review — your answer vs. crowd distribution, and the same pattern tag in a new instance later.
Modes worth having:
- Daily drill: 10 mixed questions, 5 minutes, spaced repetition.
- Targeted drill: one pattern family or timeframe.
- Exam/gauntlet: 50 questions, no feedback until the end.
- Speed round: rapid-fire, chart grid, keyboard shortcuts.
- Replay: step candle-by-candle and predict each next bar.
- Live weekly challenge: current market, resolves automatically, leaderboard.
- Head-to-head: two users, same chart, faster/more calibrated answer wins.
- Pattern wiki: public reference pages that double as SEO.
Mobile needs full-screen charts and a bottom-sheet answer panel; chart gestures fighting page scroll is a real usability trap.
6. Progression and analytics
The retention engine is a skill profile, not XP alone:
- Accuracy and calibration per pattern tag, timeframe, asset class, and regime.
- Radar chart: structure, candlesticks, volume, momentum, risk.
- Calibration curve: “when you say 70%, how often are you right?”
- “Your edge vs. baseline” with confidence intervals.
- Leakage report: tags where you’re below 50% or overconfident.
- Spaced repetition scheduled by concept/tag, never by exact chart, to avoid memorization.
- Crowd comparison after each answer (“62% got this right”) — engaging and feeds item difficulty.
Avoid loss-framed streaks, loot-box mechanics, and “you’re on fire” language that encourages risk-taking. Trading apps have a real duty of care here.
7. Content pipeline
You cannot hand-label thousands of charts. The realistic pipeline:
1. Ingest and clean OHLCV → resample → detect candidate patterns algorithmically (pivots, RANSAC trendlines, TA-Lib candlesticks, rule-based structure).
2. Human review in an internal labeling UI: confirm tags, choose the cut bar, draw the key levels, rate ambiguity.
3. Compute outcomes (triple barrier, MFE/MAE), regime, and conditional base rates.
4. QA: duplicates, overlapping instances, look-ahead leakage, timezone/DST, bad ticks, weekend gaps.
5. Publish in versioned batches.
Important details: enforce non-overlapping instances where possible, stratify by outcome so you don’t get a 60%-up dataset that rewards “always long,” and tag difficulty from ambiguity plus crowd disagreement. Overlapping windows inflate sample size and leak the same move into multiple questions.
8. Architecture
- Frontend: Next.js/React + TradingView Lightweight Charts (Apache 2.0) for candlesticks, canvas overlay for annotations. Canvas/WebGL grid for speed rounds.
- Backend: Python/FastAPI for the data and labeling pipeline; Node for the app API if you prefer one language. Postgres + TimescaleDB for bars; Redis for sessions/cache; object storage for snapshots.
- Pattern instance model: store pointers to bar ranges, not giant JSON blobs, at scale. Materialize context/reveal server-side per request.
- Real-time: WebSocket only for live/multiplayer. Don’t build it for MVP.
- Auth/payments: Stripe, standard SaaS.
- PWA: daily packs pre-downloaded for offline drilling.
Don’t render charts server-side with Puppeteer unless you need shareable images; interactive client-side charts are better and cheaper.
9. Data sources and costs
- Crypto: Binance/Kraken/Coinbase public historical klines are the easiest, cheapest start. Check terms for commercial use.
- Forex/metals: this is where it gets annoying. Dukascopy tick data, HistData, Twelve Data, Polygon, OANDA, or a broker feed. Prices differ by provider, so pick one and stay consistent. Metals spot vs. futures is another decision.
- Licensing: TradingView images and many “free” datasets are not licensed for commercial redistribution. Budget for this; it’s a real line item.
Start with 3–5 symbols and 2 timeframes. Depth beats breadth for the first year.
10. Business model, legal, ethics
- Freemium: free daily drill + limited pattern library; paid analytics, full library, live challenges, exam mode.
- B2B: white-label for prop firms and trading academies — often better economics than consumers.
- Affiliate/broker links: tempting, but conflicts with “education, not signals.” Proceed carefully.
- Legal: “educational purposes only,” hypothetical-performance disclaimers, no real-money trading, age gate, GDPR/CCPA, app-store review for trading/gambling content. Real-money prizes or leaderboard payouts invite gambling regulation.
- Ethics: session limits, reality checks, no dark patterns. This audience is already prone to over-trading.
11. Main risks
1. Noisy ground truth → users learn superstition. Mitigate with triple barriers, base rates, and calibration.
2. Memorization → charts become trivia. Mitigate with anonymization, flips, placebos, fresh data.
3. Look-ahead leakage → the app is quietly cheating. Mitigate with strict server-side reveals and pipeline QA.
4. Content cost → labeling doesn’t scale. Mitigate with detection + human review + crowd difficulty.
5. Retention → novelty fades. Mitigate with adaptive drills, fresh instances, live challenges, community.
6. Regulatory/ethical → fines or harm. Mitigate with disclaimers, no real money, responsible design.
12. If I were scoping the MVP
- 3 crypto symbols (BTC, ETH, SOL), 1h and 4h.
- 12 pattern tags, ~1,000 curated non-overlapping instances.
- Two question types: probability of upper barrier first, and trade/pass.
- Reveal with annotations + historical base rate.
- Skill dashboard with calibration and edge-vs-baseline.
- Daily drill + targeted drill, web-only PWA.
Then add forex/metals, identification and validity questions, live weekly challenges, and B2B white-label.
13. Decisions I’d want from you
1. Audience: casual beginners, serious retail, or prop-firm candidates? That changes the grading rigor and price point.
2. Priority: fun arcade game or statistically honest trainer? I’d argue the latter is the moat, but the former drives retention.
3. Scope: breadth across assets/patterns first, or depth in one market (e.g., XAU/USD + structure/ICT) first?
4. Live data in v1, or historical only? I’d defer live.
5. Business: consumer subscription, B2B white-label, or free/community?
6. Team/budget: solo build or funded? Data licensing alone can dictate the roadmap.
My strongest opinion: the differentiator isn’t “more patterns.” It’s calibration training, negative controls, and transparent base rates — treating patterns as probabilistic edges rather than magic answers.
